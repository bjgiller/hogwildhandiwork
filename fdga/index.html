A Partnership Proposal for
Walker Park & Kessler Park

Fayetteville Disc Golf Association — a 501(c)(3) nonprofit

Through a member-funded capital campaign, FDGA has raised $18,538.94 for course improvements at Walker Park's Waxhaws Disc Golf Course — at no cost to the City. As a next phase, we're proposing a new course at Kessler Park that reuses the Walker Park baskets this upgrade replaces, built with volunteer labor.

See the proposal ↓

Fayetteville Disc Golf Association is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to growing disc golf in Fayetteville — maintaining public courses, running community tournaments, and providing programming for players of all skill levels. We do this work regardless of the outcome of this proposal; we're asking the City to partner with us in doing it better.

01 — The Gift

FDGA's Capital Campaign for Walker Park

$18,538.94 raised from multiple FDGA member donations, earmarked for capital improvements at Walker Park's Waxhaws Disc Golf Course — facilitated entirely through FDGA, at zero cost to the City. A new course at Kessler Park would follow as the next phase, reusing the baskets this upgrade replaces. Once installed, every new basket and sleeve is City property, just like the existing baskets.

19

New Baskets — MVP Black Hole® Portal

18 hole baskets plus 1 practice basket, at $799.95 each — $15,199.05 total. A 4-ring, 36-chain catch system engineered to disperse kinetic energy from both soft and firm putts, PDGA championship approved, with a 20-year warranty against rust and corrosion. Royal blue — a high-visibility color that is easier for road traffic to spot and easier for new players to find, on top of the improved catch performance.

Product: MVP Black Hole® Portal, MVP Pro Shop

11

New Hole Positions — Permanent Ground Sleeves

Eleven hot-dipped galvanized steel ground sleeves, at $39.99 each — $439.89 total. Each sleeve lets a basket be relocated between multiple permanent pin positions, so course layouts can rotate throughout the year — keeping the course fresh for regular players and giving FDGA more flexibility when designing tournament layouts.

Product: Permanent Ground Sleeve (Hot-Dipped Galvanized), MVP Pro Shop

Extended Tee Pads

$2,000 donated toward concrete work extending the existing tee pads at Walker Park, improving footing and durability for high-traffic tees.

18

New Tee Signs

18 tee signs at $50 each — $900 total. FDGA is funding and commissioning the design and installation of new tee signage across the course.

Professional Course Design — Kessler Park

FDGA is sponsoring The Disc Golf Dojo — the same local outfit already contracted by the City for disc golf programming — to professionally design the new Kessler layout, at $1,000 per 9 holes, so it's built right the first time.

02 — Already In The Plan

The City's Own Plan Already Agrees

None of this is a new idea. The City's own 2023 Parks System Master Plan — built on years of resident survey data — already documents the need for more disc golf, in the City's own numbers and in residents' own words.

2 → 3

A documented shortfall

The Plan counts 2 disc golf courses citywide today — 1 per 46,975 residents, one of the lowest-served facility types the City tracks. Its own 2040 gap analysis says Fayetteville needs 3 just to hold today's level of service as the city grows: a shortfall of at least one course, already.

Source: Table 9, PDF p. 44 & Table 14, p. 99

#1

The most-mentioned ask

In the Plan's open-participation survey (461 respondents), "disc golf" was the single most common topic in open-ended comments on what to improve — 18%, ahead of natural-space preservation, requests for more parks, and every other category.

Source: Table 48, PDF p. 358

Already a recognized partner

03 — The Ask

A New Course at Kessler Park

We believe that by removing only invasive privet and honeysuckle — and cutting zero mature trees — we can create a Gold-level disc golf course at Kessler Park, reusing the City's existing Walker Park baskets once the Waxhaws upgrade replaces them. Nothing goes to waste.

Built around your existing plans

FDGA has identified wooded areas of the park for the course. We don't know all of the City's plans for Kessler, but we're committed to working around them — the course will be laid out so that whatever the City has planned can still happen. We'd welcome the City's input on where a course fits best alongside those plans.

Fairways will be routed away from trails, pavilions, the sports fields, and parking, so players and other park visitors stay out of each other's way.

The purple highlighted areas on the map below are the areas we'd like to use for the course. They're open to the City's feedback before anything is finalized.

A Destination Course

Great for locals, and worth the trip. A Gold-level course would be a great home for Fayetteville's current players and a welcoming place for new ones to grow into the sport — and it would make Kessler a course that players travel to. Visiting players and tournament fields stay in Fayetteville hotels, eat in local restaurants, and bring hotel tax revenue to the City along with them.

And a major is already on its way. The PDGA has chosen Northwest Arkansas to host the 2028 PDGA Tim Selinske United States Masters Disc Golf Championships — one of the sport's major championships, with Fayetteville named among the host cities. A Gold-level course at Kessler would put Fayetteville in position to welcome those players and the visitors who follow them, and to keep drawing events long after 2028. (PDGA announcement, Jan. 2026)

Already Proven at Kessler

FDGA's Ice Bowl already brings players to Kessler Mountain. Every year, FDGA sets up a temporary course at Kessler for its Ice Bowl charity tournament, which draws 100+ players. For each of the last five years, the Ice Bowl has raised $6,000+ for the NWA Children's Shelter (now Everhope). A permanent course would give that event, and others like it, a home.

After It Opens

We'll help it succeed after it opens. FDGA will help onboard new players at Kessler with intro clinics and course-launch events, and will keep the course clean with regular volunteer cleanup days — trash pickup, invasive removal, and basket upkeep — so it's a well-cared-for addition to the park, not another item on the City's list.

And over the long haul. FDGA will handle routine upkeep and lead fundraising for repairs and replacements as the course ages — baskets, signs, and tee pads — just as we've done with this campaign for Walker Park.

Kessler was already on residents' list. In the City's own community engagement summary for the Master Plan, Disc Golf was named among the top requested uses for Kessler Mountain Regional Park specifically (14 responses, PDF p. 235).

And residents already asked for this siting approach. “Building and Development of Disc Golf Courses in non-flood plain areas so we can focus on ongoing development of fairways… without the constant setbacks due to flooding” — Fayetteville resident, Master Plan survey, p. 321. That's exactly the wooded, non-floodplain site FDGA is proposing here.

04 — Our Part

Volunteer Labor

FDGA volunteers will help clear the site and build the course.

Clearing the site

FDGA volunteers will help clear invasive privet and honeysuckle along the Kessler fairways — zero mature trees cut. We'd walk the site with City staff before any work begins.

Building the course

FDGA will help design and build the course with The Disc Golf Dojo — pouring the concrete tee pads, putting up tee signs, and installing the baskets.

05 — Next Steps

What We're Asking For

  1. 1

    Accept the gift

    FDGA's $18,538.94 capital campaign for Walker Park: 19 new baskets, 11 new hole positions, extended tee pads, and new tee signage — installed by FDGA at no cost to the City.

  2. 2

    Land to build a Gold-level course at Kessler Park

    Built by FDGA volunteers reusing the City's existing Walker Park baskets, sited around the City's plans for the park, with FDGA also sponsoring a professional course designer ($1,000 per 9 holes).

How It Would Roll Out

Phase 1 — Walker Park. Once the City accepts the gift, FDGA installs the new baskets, sleeves, tee pad extensions, and signage, coordinated with Parks staff.

Phase 2 — Kessler design. The Disc Golf Dojo finalizes a layout, which goes through the City's normal review and approval process before anything happens on the ground.

Phase 3 — Kessler build. With approval in hand, FDGA volunteers prep the site and install the retired Walker Park baskets.
