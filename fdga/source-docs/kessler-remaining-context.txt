Fayetteville and Recreation
With a mission to "meet the needs of all by providing a safe and diversified park system that encourages community
pride, visionary planning and operations, and environmental stewardship", the City of Fayetteville supports
recreation for its residents and visitors in a variety of ways through parks and open space providing space for active
and passive recreation offerings. Much of Fayetteville's properties focus on recreational opportunities that are
desirable and compatible with the open spaces and natural settings. These include passive recreation activities, like
wildlife watching, walking, and biking which can offer constructive, restorative, and enjoyable opportunities
outdoors.

Today, Fayetteville's parks, open spaces, and trails offer a broad variety of outdoor recreation activities that are
growing in diversity. Some of the recreational offerings include picnic areas, biking and mountain biking trails,
playgrounds, basketball, volleyball, horseshoes, disc golf, softball, boating, fishing, and many more. Residents and
visitors can enjoy over 80 miles of natural surface trails and boardwalks, 27 playgrounds, 7 tennis courts, 7 volleyball
courts, 3 lakes, a swimming pool, new splashpad, skate park, plus many more recreational opportunities. The City's
Parks and Recreation Department hosts a plethora of events most notable being the Gulley Park Summer Concert
Series and the Homegrown Music Festival at Kessler Mountain. Mountain bike trails are also becoming a popular
venue for mountain biking events.1

Fayetteville's City Plan 2040 also seeks in its goals to assemble an Enduring Green Network (EGN) that vigilantly
nurtures a continuum of greenspace and helps the city strategically acquire land to incorporate into the Network.
The EGN aims to include major parks, natural areas, trails, and other facilities that will offer recreation prospects.
The Plan also has two designated land uses relevant for outdoor recreation, "Natural Areas" and "Civic and Private
Open Space/Parklands". The first is set to guide areas that are less urbanized and enjoy more wilderness conditions;
the guiding policies seek to preserve and conserve whilst encouraging recreational and educational programing. The
latter land use is broader in definition and policies as it seeks encourage a plethora of open space and parkland
activities.

The City has different partnerships that help them better engage their community and more successfully utilize their
assets to offer the best recreational opportunities. Some of the partners include the Botanical Gardens of the
Ozarks, the Horseshoe Association, and the Senior Activity & Wellness Center. There are also a few clubs that the
city is working with like the Ozark Volleyball Club, Ozark Flyers Club, Rowing Club, and the Boys & Girls Club that
offers services to children and families in the city. Finally, their partnership with Public Schools that has been
ongoing for several years has also led to successful outcomes like joint lease agreements and shared facilities.
